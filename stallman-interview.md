# Interview with Richard Stallman

This page preserves a historical interview with Richard Stallman, founder of the GNU Project and the Free Software Foundation, originally conducted by the Iranian Linux magazine *Linux Review*. The interview was later translated into English and republished internationally, reaching a global audience interested in software freedom and digital rights.

The conversation covers fundamental topics in free software philosophy, including the distinction between "free software" and "open source," the importance of calling the operating system "GNU/Linux" rather than just "Linux," and the technical and ethical motivations behind GPLv3. Stallman also addresses the impact of sanctions on software freedom and the universal nature of the free software movement, emphasizing that software freedom belongs to all people regardless of geography or politics.

The original *Linux Review* website is no longer online. This repository preserves a reference to the interview for historical and educational purposes, ensuring that this important discussion remains accessible to researchers, students, and advocates of software freedom.

## Topics Covered

- **The four freedoms of free software** – the ethical foundation of the free software movement
- **GNU vs Linux naming** – why the operating system should be called GNU/Linux
- **GPLv3 and tivoization** – protecting user freedom from hardware restrictions
- **Free software vs open source philosophy** – the distinction between ethical and practical approaches
- **Software freedom and global access** – how sanctions and restrictions threaten universal software freedom
