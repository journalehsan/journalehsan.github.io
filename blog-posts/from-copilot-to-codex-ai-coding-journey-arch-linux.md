# 💻 From Copilot to Codex: My AI Coding Journey on Arch Linux 🐧

There are moments in tech that change the way you feel about programming.
For me, it started with a blinking cursor and a strange new tool called **GitHub Copilot**.

---

## 🚀 The Beginning — When Autocomplete Felt Like Magic

I still remember the day Copilot was announced. It looked surreal — a model that could write code with you.
It wasn’t even available to everyone yet, but I requested access and, just one day later, got in.

The first time I saw it complete an entire function, I literally whispered, “What?!” 😄
The official GIF on GitHub’s page showing Copilot filling in whole lines felt like a sneak peek into the future.

Back then, it was free. Just magic, pure and simple.

---

## 🧭 The Middle Years — The Age of AI Tools

When Copilot became paid, I didn’t hesitate — I subscribed immediately.
But curiosity pushed me further: I started exploring every emerging AI dev tool.

- **Cody (Sourcegraph)** — amazing at context, and they even gave me a free Pro account for a while. When they sunset it, they left a $10 AMP credit as a goodbye gift 💛
- **Cursor** — a new kind of IDE that made AI feel native.
- **Warp** — the terminal that turned command lines into chat.

Each tool had its own personality, strengths, and quirks.
And I kept switching, testing, learning — chasing that “perfect AI partner.”

---

## ⚙️ Now — Living with Codex

Then came **Codex**, and everything changed again.
It wasn’t just completing code — it was understanding architecture.

Today, I use Codex across all my projects — from **Rust + Iced clients** to **Laravel backends**, even for Markdown blog generation.
It’s like having a quiet teammate who reads the repo, gets the logic, fixes warnings, and politely refactors code without drama 😌

And the wild part? I often use the **Medium model**, and it already feels like a senior dev.
I can only imagine what the High model will be like in 2026… probably less like autocomplete and more like co-creation.

---

## 🐧 The Arch Linux Chapter — Samba, Simplified

Today added another “wow” to the list.
I finally mounted our **Samba share directly on Arch Linux** using **smb4k**, without VMware Horizon, without any weird detours.

Now I can copy `.msi` builds straight to our shared folder — natively, cleanly, Linux-style.
Small victory? Maybe. But it felt like reclaiming simplicity.

No hypervisor, no middleman — just me, my system, and my code.

---

## 💬 Reflection — From Magic to Mastery

My AI journey started with curiosity and a GIF.
Now, it’s a daily collaboration between human intuition and machine precision.

From Copilot’s first spark ✨
To Codex’s full understanding 🧠
To smb4k quietly mounting my world into `/mnt/share` 🐧

Every step has made me realize:
We’re not automating creativity. We’re amplifying it.
