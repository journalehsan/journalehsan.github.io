# 52 Days with Dynabook K50/FP on Omarchy

## A small machine that refused to be small

Fifty‑two days ago, I installed Omarchy on a Toshiba Dynabook K50/FP. No dual‑boot safety net, no backup laptop waiting on the desk. Just a 10‑inch device, Arch‑based Linux, and curiosity.

What followed was not an experiment that needed excuses. It became daily reality.

---

## Hardware reality check

The Dynabook K50/FP is not built to impress spec sheets:

- Intel Celeron N4000
- Integrated Intel UHD Graphics
- 10‑inch display
- Fanless, low‑power design

On paper, this is the kind of device many people would dismiss instantly. In practice, it turned out to be something else entirely.

---

## Omarchy + Arch: the foundation matters

Omarchy gave this device a backbone. Arch Linux underneath keeps the system lean, predictable, and fast to boot. Nothing runs unless I explicitly want it to.

Package management stays out of the way. Updates are transparent. The system feels honest. No hidden background work draining battery or stealing cycles.

After 52 days, system age and uptime tell a simple story: stability without stagnation.

---

## Hyprland on a 10‑inch screen

This is where the magic happens.

Hyprland shines on this form factor. On a small screen, traditional desktop environments waste space. Hyprland does the opposite. Every pixel has intent.

- Tiling feels natural instead of restrictive
- Animations stay smooth even on low‑power hardware
- Gestures turn the device into a hybrid tablet‑laptop

The result is focus. One or two windows at a time. No clutter. No friction.

---

## Real battery life, not marketing hours

With around half screen brightness and real usage, the Dynabook delivers close to **8 hours of battery life**.

Not idle time. Not airplane mode.

Actual work:

- Web browsing
- YouTube
- Coding sessions
- Vibe coding workflows

For a device of this size and age, this is a quiet win.

---

## Development on lightweight hardware

This machine is not meant for massive builds, and that is fine.

What it excels at:

- React development
- Django projects
- Lightweight Rust development
- Debugging and iteration

When a task needs heavy lifting, modern tools step in. Codex, Lovable, and AI‑assisted workflows offload complexity. The Dynabook becomes the thinking terminal, not the furnace.

This balance makes it surprisingly productive.

---

## Vibe coding, anywhere

The portability changes behavior. I code more casually, more often.

Short sessions add up:

- Quick feature tweaks
- Reviewing diffs
- Writing prompts
- Debugging logic

The device invites usage instead of demanding preparation.

---

## What this setup proves

After 52 days, one conclusion stands out:

Low‑power hardware paired with the right Linux stack is still powerful.

Not in benchmarks. In momentum.

Hyprland gives structure. Omarchy removes noise. Arch provides control. The Dynabook quietly delivers.

---

## Final thoughts

The Dynabook K50/FP running Omarchy is not trying to replace a workstation. It does something more interesting.

It stays ready.

For travel, casual development, focused writing, and modern AI‑assisted workflows, this setup works better than it has any right to.

Fifty‑two days in, I no longer think of it as a temporary experiment. It earned its place.

Sometimes the best machines are the ones that simply let you work.
